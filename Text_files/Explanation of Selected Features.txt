Explanation of the 25 Selected Features

1. Flow_Duration

Measures how long a network flow lasts.
Why kept: Many attacks (DoS, scanning, slowloris) show abnormal flow length.

---

2. Dst_Port

Represents the destination port.
Why kept: Attacks often target specific ports (e.g., 80, 443, 22), making it a strong signature.

---

3. Flow_IAT_Min

Minimum inter-arrival time between packets.
Why kept: Very small IAT indicates high-speed attacks or bursts of malicious traffic.

---

4. Flow_IAT_Mean

Average packet arrival time in a flow.
Why kept: Detects abnormal timing behavior, common in automated attacks.

---

5. Flow_IAT_Max

Maximum gap between packets.
Why kept: Helps identify slow attacks or idle behaviors.

---

6. Flow_Pkts/s

Number of packets per second.
Why kept: High packet rate is a common signature of DoS and brute-force attacks.

---

7. Flow_Byts/s

Bytes transmitted per second.
Why kept: Detects high-volume attacks or excessive data exfiltration.

---

8. Pkt_Len_Max

Largest packet size in the flow.
Why kept: Malicious payloads often use unusually large packets.

---

9. Pkt_Len_Mean

Average packet size in a flow.
Why kept: Benign and malicious traffic often differ in average packet size.

---

10. Pkt_Size_Avg

Average size of packets including header information.
Why kept: Good for detecting payload patterns used by malware.

---

11. Pkt_Len_Min

Smallest packet size.
Why kept:Very small packets often occur in scanning or probing attacks.

---

12. Pkt_Len_Std

Standard deviation of packet lengths.
Why kept:Measures variability. Attacks have either uniform or highly irregular packet sizes.

---

13. Idle_Min

Minimum idle time between packet bursts.
Why kept: Near-zero idle time indicates continuous attacking behavior.

---

14. Idle_Mean

Average idle time in a flow.
Why kept:Used to detect flows with unusual silence patterns (e.g., stealthy attacks).

---

15. Idle_Max

Longest idle gap in the flow.
Why kept:Identifies slow-paced attacks such as slowloris.

---

16. Fwd_Pkts/s

Forward packet rate.
Why kept: High rate indicates attacker-to-server floods or brute force attempts.

---

17. Bwd_Pkts/s

Backward packet rate (server response rate).
Why kept: Helps detect asymmetric flows where the attacker floods but server barely responds.

---

18. Init_Bwd_Win_Byts

Initial TCP window size in backward direction.
Why kept: Abnormal window sizes are common in crafted or spoofed packets.

---

19. ACK_Flag_Cnt

Number of ACK flags in the flow.
Why kept:ACK patterns change noticeably in SYN floods, TCP hijacking, and scanning.

---

20. SYN_Flag_Cnt

Counts SYN packets.
Why kept:Strongest indicator of SYN flood, scanning, and connection attempts.

---

21. Bwd_Header_Len

Total header size in backward direction.
Why kept:Abnormal header sizes indicate manipulation or tunneling.

---

22. Fwd_Pkt_Len_Max

Maximum size of forward packets.
Why kept: Attackers often send oversized packets carrying malicious payloads.

---

23. Fwd_Pkt_Len_Min

Minimum size of forward packets.
Why kept: Captures automation-related repetitive small packets.

---

24. Bwd_Pkt_Len_Max

Largest backward packet size.
Why kept:Responses to attacks often show abnormal packet size spikes.

---

25. Bwd_Pkt_Len_Mean

Average packet length in the backward direction.
Why kept: Helps differentiate normal server behavior from attack responses.

---



These features were selected because they:

 Repeatedly ranked high across all feature-selection methods (Correlation, ANOVA-F, Mutual Information, Decision Tree, XGBoost, SHAP)
 Represent traffic behavior, timing, rate, size, and TCP flags, which together give complete attack detection coverage
 Minimize redundancy and maximize predictive power
 Capture essential characteristics of:

   DoS/DDoS
   Port scanning
   Brute force
   Botnet traffic
   Data exfiltration
   Slow attacks
   Spoofing / abnormal TCP behavior
